## Part 1 — The macro framework

### 1.1 The one-sentence thesis

The Exponential Age is the moment intelligence stopped being scarce and became abundant. Every thematic that matters for the next decade flows from that single fact and its consequences across energy, money, labour, and physical infrastructure. The investable expression is not the intelligence itself (software, hyperscalers) but the **physical buildout required to make it abundant** — and the second-order systems that buildout forces into existence.

### 1.2 The two organising principles

**"Your CapEx is my opportunity."** The AI era inverts the prior 15-year framework. The software era rewarded asset-light, capital-light businesses. The AI era rewards whoever *receives* the capital expenditure from those same businesses. Hyperscalers are the CapEx spenders; the portfolio targets the CapEx receivers — power, grid, cooling, specialty chemicals, optical, semis equipment.

**"Speed of steel vs speed of code."** The digital world moves at the speed of code. The physical world moves at the speed of steel, copper, silicon, chemistry, energy, and regulation. That gap is the opportunity. Layer 1–3 companies operate at the speed of steel: their revenue is contracted, not multiple-dependent, so they cannot be disrupted by a software model release. This is what makes the thesis durable even when crypto loses momentum and software deteriorates.

### 1.3 Benchmark arbitrage

Cap-weighted indices (S&P 500, ACWI IMI) overweight what has already won (hyperscalers, software, consumer tech — the funding vehicles) and underweight what the thesis wants (power infrastructure, specialty chemicals, grid equipment — the CapEx receivers). Running **zero software exposure** is therefore not a tactical bet but structurally correct benchmark-arbitrage positioning for the AI era.

Adam Parker's deterioration sequence for software: multiple compression (done) → earnings miss → sales miss. Analyst assumptions of 80%+ software gross margins through 2028–29 are too static. The risk to manage: a software earnings miss can trigger broad de-risking that temporarily pulls down semis and crypto alongside it, even though their fundamentals differ.

### 1.4 Multi-frontier liquidity (Joe Bland integration)

The thesis is one interconnected industrial ecosystem, not a set of isolated stock picks. AI compute drives power demand drives energy buildout; quantum threatens security which drives sovereign spending which drives defense which drives space which loops back to connectivity for AI. Because frontier liquidity now competes across many themes simultaneously (AI, quantum, energy, photonics, space, defense, humanoids, sovereign), it no longer concentrates into a single speculative outlet. **This is the structural reason BTC's beta is lower than in 2020–2021** — the marginal speculative dollar has many more homes. Layers can rally simultaneously while a cap-weighted index looks flat.

The portfolio implication: when a research report names any node in the system map, it is implicitly confirming demand for every connected node. A report confirming AI capex acceleration is also confirming power, optical, and eventually quantum-security demand.

## Part 2 — Regime methodology

This is the engine for deciding *when* to weight each theme. It is the part to run fresh every session.

### 2.1 Three drivers, four seasons

Each driver is read as a 0–100% probability:

- **Growth** (>50 = expansion): ISM PMI, capital goods shipments, Korea semi exports, industrial production.
- **Inflation** (>50 = rising/hot): CPI YoY, PCE, PPI, breakeven rates, energy prices.
- **Liquidity** (>50 = expanding/loose): Fed balance sheet, credit spreads (HY/IG), NFCI, BTC momentum.

The drivers map to a four-season classification:

| Season | Growth / Inflation | Character | Favoured layers |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Spring** | ↑ / ↓ | Risk-on, disinflationary growth | L1/L2 most favoured |
| **Summer** | ↑ / ↑ | Late-cycle / Reflationary Boom | L2 CapEx receivers, commodities |
| **Fall** | ↓ / ↑ | Stagflation risk | Energy, L2 infrastructure as hedge |
| **Winter** | ↓ / ↓ | Defensive contraction | L4 hard assets, cash |

**Rule: do not assume a fixed current regime.** Each session, assess whether new content confirms, challenges, or upgrades the classification. The key structural advantage of the thesis: **L2 physical infrastructure is the defensible position in *both* Summer and Fall** — it is simultaneously the growth trade and the inflation hedge. That dual property is what makes underweight-L2-relative-to-L1 the exploitable institutional edge.

### 2.2 The BTC trigger (Jordi)

When CPI YoY exceeds the 3-month T-bill yield, real short rates go negative and holding cash becomes a guaranteed real loss, which forces allocation to hard assets. The trigger is **binary** — fired or not. To check: compare the latest CPI YoY print against the current 3-month T-bill yield. Never carry forward a prior "distance to trigger" estimate; recompute each session. Proximity below ~0.5pp = imminent.

> **↪ EVOLVED — trigger naming.** Earlier sessions referred to this as the "Atreides trigger." It is the same mechanism (CPI YoY > 3M T-bill). Treat the names as interchangeable; the current skill uses the plain description.

### 2.3 Shadow Monetary Base (Tipper framework)

Central bank reserves + eligible bonds. Distinct from headline Global Liquidity Index. When the Shadow MB contracts while the headline GLI rises, the headline is being inflated by a collateral multiplier, not genuine base creation — a negative Shadow-MB rate-of-change is a near-term BTC headwind even when the headline liquidity number looks bullish. **Always check both, not just the headline.** Related: the eSLR-driven private-sector repo expansion is structurally invisible to Fed-balance-sheet-only models, so "tight liquidity" readings from those frameworks are systematically incomplete.

### 2.4 Late-cycle signatures to watch

- **DGS30 Tipper flag:** 30Y Treasury yield at multi-decade highs (e.g. 2007 levels) is a late-cycle flag; combined with a bear-flattening curve it signals the Summer→Fall transition. 30Y > ~5.0% is the explicit threshold used in the dashboard.
- **Bear-flattening yield curve:** late-cycle regime signature.
- **Gold/Silver ratio:** late-cycle precious-metals signal.
- **Concentration risk:** index at highs while the median stock lags (SPY vs RSP divergence) = concentration, not breadth health. >10pp 6-month SPY–RSP spread = elevated warning.
- **Sector breadth:** fewer than ~55% of the 11 sector SPDRs above their 200-day MA = fragile breadth masking index strength. Financials (XLF) below its 200-day is a specific Jordi warning that real-economy strength isn't transmitting to markets.

### 2.5 Energy-inflation persistence

Spot oil/gas spikes can be transient; the persistence tell is **back-month futures** (e.g. September gas above spot). Elevated back months mean inflation is priced into forward contracts, not just a one-month shock — this is what turns a "manageable" energy read into a CPI-above-4% risk. Strait of Hormuz disruptions feed this directly; secondary effects propagate to helium/specialty gas (→ ENTG) on a multi-week lag.

### 3.1 Layer detail and key names

**L1 — Compute.** The physical stack to make intelligence abundant. Most crowded institutional expression; breadth narrowing. Key signal: TSMC CoWoS booked through 2026, hyperscaler backlog ~$1.3T contracted-but-undeliverable. Names: TSM, NVDA, ASML, AMAT, LRCX, KLAC; memory MU; optical/photonics MRVL, LITE, COHR (the P1→P2 transition trade). 

**L2 — Power/grid/thermal — the core.** Data centres consuming an accelerating share of electricity, reshaping the entire power system. Sub-themes: grid (transformers/switchgear, 100–150wk lead times), power management (grid→rack), thermal (liquid cooling, rack density 10kW→120kW), power semis (SiC/GaN), specialty chemicals (EUV photoresists, near-zero substitutes), nuclear/gas baseload. Key empirical anchors: power-transformer lead times ~128wk, GSU ~144wk; ~30% power-transformer supply deficit (Wood Mackenzie 2025); ~$2B committed to N. American transformer capacity (Hitachi $457M VA 2028, Siemens $150M NC 2027, Eaton $340M SC 2027); US data-centre grid demand ~61.8GW 2025 → ~134GW 2030 (451 Research). Names: ETN, VRT, GEV, CEG, VST, HUBB, PWR, EME, ON, APH, ABB.

**L3 — Robotics.** Industrial automation is real now; humanoid is pre-earnings speculation. No pure-play public humanoid; TSLA is a proxy with enormous non-robotics exposure. Realistic Optimus scale (10,000+ units) is 2028–2029, not Musk's 2027 — every Optimus timeline since 2022 has slipped, and the 2025 production target missed by >90%. Supply-chain exposure (actuators, sensors, edge compute) is more reliable than betting the manufacturer. Cleanest broad expression: RBOT (iShares Automation & Robotics UCITS, on SKAT positivliste 2025). New retail proxy for *private* humanoid: $BOT (see Part 5).

**L4 — Monetary.** BTC as neutral monetary network + agent-economy money; stablecoins as dollar-expansion vehicle. 21M hard cap, supply absorbed by long-duration holders. Trigger-gated (see 2.2). MSTR = BTC-beta, belongs in the Bitcoin basket, not Pluto. Regulatory maturation (CFTC perpetual futures, ICE/Hyperliquid) is a durable, regime-independent tailwind to file as multi-year. Names: BTC direct, MSTR, COIN, HOOD, CRCL, BITO.

**L5 — Sovereign.** Every major power building its own model/chip/data-centre stack; de-dollarisation as a decades-long process. Not winner-take-all — a multi-regional buildout that extends TAM beyond US-centric analysis. Proxies: EWY, INDA, EEM.

**L7 — Quantum.** Two converging demand drivers: QC accelerates AI discovery, *and* security fears create immediate sovereign revenue for quantum-safe firms before QC even matures (the moment a cryptographically-relevant QC exists, all current encryption is retroactively broken). High volatility, mostly pre-revenue. Names: IONQ, QBTS (commercial revenue earliest, via annealing), RGTI, ARQQ, LAES.

**L8 — Space.** A connectivity-infrastructure story, not a science story — satellite broadband extends AI reach to the 99% of the planet without terrestrial fibre; secure satcom is a defense imperative. SpaceX is private; RKLB is the most liquid public proxy with the clearest revenue path. The public SpaceX IPO prospectus is a sector re-rating catalyst now on a determinable timeline. Names: RKLB, ASTS, LUNR.

**L9 — Industrial hard tech & defense.** Physical reindustrialisation driven by AI fab demand + rearmament + the end of offshoring. Sovereign investment (NATO 2%, CHIPS Act, EU Sovereignty Fund) provides regime-insensitive demand that *grows in recession*. This is the new defensive — not bonds or utilities, but strategic hard tech with government contract backing. Names: KTOS (autonomous drones/hypersonics), AXON, LDOS, ABB (grid+robotics dual), PLTR (government moat, lower thesis fit).

### 3.2 The cycle-phase sub-framework

Within the layer cake runs a phase model answering *when* to weight each theme. Decision logic: EPS surprises → identify cycle phase → tilt theme weights → set name weights. **The key rule: track the *next* bottleneck, not the current one** — by the time consensus names a bottleneck (GPU scarcity 2024), the trade has run. Phase 1 (2024–26): secure compute — GPU/HBM/packaging. Phase 2 (2026–28): system scale — optics (800G/1.6T), rack deployments, cooling intensity. Phase 3: energy/power as the binding constraint. Silicon photonics (LITE, COHR) is the explicit P1→P2 transition trade.

### 3.3 The three-bucket overlay

Cutting across the layers, every position belongs to one of three buckets with different exit rules:

- **Core** — real present-income companies. Exit only on fundamental/macro grounds; exhaustion signals used only for DCA pacing, never as a sell trigger.
- **Satellite** — future-income speculation / price proxies. Full technical stops apply.
- **Bitcoin** — its own ruleset, trigger-gated. MSTR sits here as BTC-beta, not in Pluto.

The membership test: *"does this company have real present income, or is it speculating on future income?"* COIN/HOOD/CRCL = core; miners = satellite; MSTR = Bitcoin basket.
